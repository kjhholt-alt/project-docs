# 03 — Episode Template & Backlog

## Per-episode folder structure

Every episode lives in its own folder so the pipeline is repeatable:

```
episodes/
  ep01-the-christmas-truce/
    EPISODE.md          # the brief (this template, filled in)
    research/
      SOURCES.md        # every fact + citation, human-checked
      notes.md
    script/
      outline.md        # act map from STYLE_SPEC
      narration.md      # the script w/ [SILENCE]/[SCORE]/[B-ROLL] cues
    production/
      shot-list.md      # narration beat -> required visual
      edl.md            # edit decision list
      music-cues.md
    package/
      titles.md         # 3-5 options
      description.md     # w/ sources + chapters
      thumbnail-brief.md
    scorecard.md        # post-publish retention/CTR/feedback -> feeds STYLE_SPEC
```

## `EPISODE.md` template

```markdown
# Episode [N]: [Title]

## One-line hook
[The single sentence that makes someone click and stay.]

## Through-line
[The recurring human anchor: a soldier, a letter, a place, an object.]

## Why this story (the "human moment")
[The intimate entry point we open on, before the strategic pullback.]

## Strategic significance
[Why it mattered to the war — the pullback context.]

## Target length
[e.g. 28 min]

## Act map (from STYLE_SPEC)
- Cold open (0:00–1:30): [hook]
- Act 1 — setup (1:30–9:00): [...]
- Act 2 — escalation (9:00–20:00): [...]
- Act 3 — cost & meaning (20:00–28:00): [...]
- Landing: [the final image / name / number / silence]

## Tone notes
[Anything specific to this episode's emotional register.]

## Sourcing risk
[Flag any claims that are contested among historians.]
```

## Starter backlog — first 6 episodes

These are chosen for strong **human anchors** + **public-domain visual coverage** +
**under-served on YouTube**. Confirm/adjust during research.

| # | Working title | Human anchor | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Christmas Truce, 1914** | Soldiers who climbed out of the trenches | Famous but rarely told *well* long-form; huge emotional pull; great cold open |
| 2 | **Verdun: The Mincing Machine** | One French *poilu*'s rotation through the line | The Carlin-style scale-of-horror episode; defining WWI battle |
| 3 | **The Harlem Hellfighters** | Henry Johnson | Under-told, strong characters, US angle, great archival photos |
| 4 | **Gallipoli: The Wrong Beach** | An ANZAC landing soldier | Built-in tragedy + geography; big AU/NZ/UK audience |
| 5 | **The Red Baron & the birth of air war** | Manfred von Richthofen | Character-driven, broad appeal, lots of imagery |
| 6 | **The Last Day: Nov 11, 1918** | A soldier killed in the final hours | Devastating landing episode; the meaning-of-it-all piece |

> Pick **Episode 1 (Christmas Truce)** as the pipeline shakedown — it's emotionally
> self-contained, visually well-covered, and forgiving to get the *feel* right.

## The 12-point ship checklist (pull from STYLE_SPEC once filled)

Before any episode publishes, it must pass the checklist generated by the teardown,
e.g.:
1. Cold open hooks within 90s via a human or a question
2. At least N human↔strategic cycles at the spec's rhythm
3. Narration WPM within spec range; slows on emotional beats
4. ≥1 score-only breath roughly every 90s
5. Emotion shown, never editorialized
6. Every on-screen claim traces to `SOURCES.md`
7. AI-generated visuals labeled; no fake-archival
8. Through-line recurs and pays off at the landing
9. Maps used sparingly per spec
10. Description has sources + chapters
11. Title is curiosity + clarity, not clickbait-lie
12. Final fact spot-check passed by a human
