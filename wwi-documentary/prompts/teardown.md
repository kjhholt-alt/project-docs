# Prompt — Style Teardown

> Run this against a **timestamped transcript** of a reference documentary.
> Output is a reusable *Style Spec* (patterns + rules), NEVER a copy of content.

---

You are a documentary craft analyst. You are given a timestamped transcript of a
documentary I admire. Your job is to reverse-engineer **how it works** so I can
reproduce the *style* in my own original films about a different topic.

CRITICAL RULES:
- Extract **patterns, techniques, and rules** only. Do NOT reproduce or lightly
  paraphrase the source's sentences. If you quote, quote at most 5 words and only
  to illustrate a *technique*, clearly marked as an example of a move (not content
  to reuse).
- Be specific and quantitative wherever possible.

Analyze the transcript on these axes and fill in the Style Spec template:

A. NARRATION VOICE — words-per-minute (estimate from timestamps), sentence-length
   mix, person/tense rules and when they switch, vocabulary register, use of
   silence, and how it handles emotion (shown vs. told).

B. STORY ARCHITECTURE — the cold-open formula and its length, the rhythm of the
   "human anchor ↔ strategic pullback" cycle (give approximate durations), the act
   structure, the through-line device, the mid-point turn, and how it lands.

C. EXPERT/HISTORIAN USAGE — what is reserved for an expert's voice vs. the
   narrator; catalog specific rhetorical moves (sensory immersion, "imagine you are
   there," scale-of-horror framing, rhetorical questions).

D. SOUND & IMAGE (inferred) — note any cues in the transcript about pacing,
   silence, and emphasis that imply edit/score discipline.

Then OUTPUT:
1. A filled-in `STYLE_SPEC.md` using the template in 01_STYLE_TEARDOWN.md.
2. A concrete **12-point checklist** any new script must pass to match this style.
3. A short list of the 5 most distinctive, reproducible techniques.
